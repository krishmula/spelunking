# GPU kernels and utilization

Five lab-authored papers illustrate three distinct kinds of evidence. A paper can identify follow-up work, rely on an assumption, or delimit its evaluation. The third column gives the inference drawn for this investigation. The G identifiers refer to the adjacent source ledger.

| Paper and section | What the authors say or assume | Implication after checking later work |
|---|---|---|
| FlashAttention-3, Meta, NVIDIA, Colfax, Princeton, Georgia Tech and Together AI, §4.2 and §5 (G3) | FP16 had a persistent kernel and load balancing in their benchmarks. FP8 did not. Their numerical stress test injected outliers into synthetic inputs. They called out inference optimization, FP8 persistence and large-scale training accuracy as future work. | “Optimize inference” is too broad. FlashInfer and FlashAttention-4 already contain serving and scheduling mechanisms. A current sparse MLA backend goes further by preparing capacity-specific plans at startup (G9). | FlashAttention-4, Meta, NVIDIA, Colfax, Princeton, Georgia Tech and Together AI, §3.3, §5 and §6 (G1) | For variable request lengths the paper sorts by the maximum estimated tile time, and describes the sort as free when its metadata can be cached. ## Candidate for hostile review

**When does sorting pay for itself in dense paged attention?** Under continuous batching, can a simple per-step choice of longest-work-first or existing request order improve p95 TPOT at fixed offered load relative to the best fixed order and current engine default? Keep admissions and the sequence of logical batches fixed in a timing replay, then check the result under live arrivals. This is our inference from G1 and G2. The unresolved quantity is saved GPU time across *all* eligible attention layers minus the exposed cost of building and applying the permutation once. Reuse across layers is part of the baseline. Reusing stale sequence lengths or old slot assignments is invalid.

**Reasons to reject.** PersistentKV (§3.9 and §5.1, G7) already evaluates adaptive dispatch against FlashInfer with wall time that includes planning. Its authors explicitly call for real traces and model-level testing. Tangram (§2.2.3 and §4, G6) already challenges cross-layer reuse under nonuniform head budgets. Sparse MLA in vLLM preplans by capacity (G9). A vLLM PR found a host sync during speculative preparation whose reported benefit shrank after rebasing (G10). A sorting-only speedup measured on a stale backend or a one-layer benchmark would establish little. The specific FA4 paper path must be confirmed active on the pinned backend before attributing any effect to it.

**Cheapest test.** On one compatible Hopper or Blackwell GPU, start with current version-pinned kernels on identical inputs and separate timing for preparing the mapping, attention across a model's actual layer count, and the synchronized whole forward pass. Vary request replacement frequency at fixed length histogram, then vary length spread at fixed replacement frequency. Include batch 1 and equal lengths as negative controls. Preserve logical output order and compare outputs against an unsorted reference. If the measured per-step oracle of the two legal choices cannot improve whole-model p95 TPOT by 3% against the best fixed policy on two held-out trace windows at equal arrival rate and no p95 TTFT regression, stop. The threshold is a proposed screening heuristic. Colab assignment and hardware access for this path are unconfirmed. The local Mac can replay membership and estimate lifetimes only. No measurements were performed.

## Search coverage

Searches combined “FA4 varlen sort metadata caching”, “schedule reuse between decode steps”, “FlashInfer plan overhead”, “POD attention prefill decode overlap” and “FA4 vLLM scheduler metadata”. Reviewed primary papers, a PyTorch maintainer account, vLLM backend documentation, a vLLM PR and the public FlashInfer API. No conclusion about a fleet-wide gain, sparse attention, AMD accelerators or remaining FP8 training questions follows from this sample.

**Implementation check.** In the upstream code inspected on November 17, the CuTe metadata helper explicitly rejects its LPT batch-sort option (G13). The Hopper C++ preparation kernel implements both branches, including a merge sort and a virtual-to-actual batch map (G14). Start on Hopper by exposing that existing internal choice in a pinned build and holding split counts and all other metadata rules fixed. The FA4 paper describes an algorithm, while the tested code path determines the claim that any experiment could support. A Blackwell test using external permutation must charge for creating, applying and inverting it.
